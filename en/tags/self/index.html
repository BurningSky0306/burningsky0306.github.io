The Price of Becoming Myself

There is a certain kind of person. They see through the rules by which the world runs earlier than the rest of us, and move through the power fields of human relations with an almost instinctive polish. They know how to present themselves, they excel at socializing, and they calibrate every interaction as an investment in future resources. I have to admit that I envy them. The envy comes from a deep comparison with myself. I was shaped in a world of high certainty, where the rules were clear, the path was singular and evaluation was objective. The logic of solving exam problems became my instinct: rely on ability, believe in open competition, trust that the judgment of worth should in the end come back to talent and learning themselves. It protected my sincerity and built my rationality. But once I step into the gray areas beyond the rules, this logic shows how fragile it is. I am clumsy at scheming and planning, and too ashamed to actively manage myself as a resource. And so I see them. They have set aside the sense of shame I hold dear, and they move with ease through the very situations in which I hesitate. I envy that capacity to act, that courage to face desire head-on and throw oneself at a goal with everything one has. But the other side of envy is a deeper wariness: wariness of the way such efficiency erodes the pure pursuit of learning, wariness of the arrogance and hypocrisy that may breed beneath such polish.

At the Threshold of the Torrent

Today is my seventh day at Shenzhen University. This campus — and Shenzhen behind it — is presenting me with a set of stark paradoxes. SZU has world-class resources: opportunities within arm’s reach, generous funding, a transport network seamlessly stitched into the city. And yet, at the most basic level of everyday experience, it shows a rough edge: canteens that come nowhere near those of Tianjin Normal University, where I did my undergraduate degree; a campus network wildly out of keeping with the reputation of this hub of innovation; and roads made dangerous by the absence of any planned lanes for bikes and scooters.
