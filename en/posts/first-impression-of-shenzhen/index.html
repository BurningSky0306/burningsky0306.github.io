At the Threshold of the Torrent

Jethro Vanbrook & Gemini 2.5 ProSeptember 24, 2025 Translated with Claude Opus 5.5

Today is my seventh day at Shenzhen University. This campus — and Shenzhen behind it — is presenting me with a set of stark paradoxes.

SZU has world-class resources: opportunities within arm’s reach, generous funding, a transport network seamlessly stitched into the city. And yet, at the most basic level of everyday experience, it shows a rough edge: canteens that come nowhere near those of Tianjin Normal University, where I did my undergraduate degree; a campus network wildly out of keeping with the reputation of this hub of innovation; and roads made dangerous by the absence of any planned lanes for bikes and scooters.

This rupture between the grand narrative and the lived detail was my very first feel for SZU, and for Shenzhen.

For me, my first impressions of the university come down to two words.

The first is imprint. Shenzhen University’s visual identity is etched, pervasively, onto everything in sight. It is on the guardrails of the dorm beds, in the scratches on the canteen trays, on the fronts of the Liyuan shuttle buses that weave across campus, on the glass railings of the footbridge spanning the Yuehai and Canghai campuses — it is present at a density that brooks no argument — an almost obsessive insistence on identity, far beyond anything I have seen at any other university. It is not merely branding but a confirmation of identity, as if reminding everyone at SZU, at every moment: you belong here; you are being absorbed into a vast and young community.

The second is flux. Here, all things, ideas and relationships become antiquated before they can ossify; the new ones finish iterating and replacing the old before the old have found their footing, and the new announce, at the very moment of their birth, their own fate of swift extinction and replacement. Here, the value of experience is worn away; adapting to change itself is the only law of survival.

These two impressions are the two sides of a single spiritual core. The imprint is a root and a sense of belonging, artificially implanted in a land without historical baggage — an attempt to fix a solid coordinate amid unpredictable tides. Flux, for its part, is written into the city’s DNA: it is the powerful force that keeps driving it forward.

Whatever the case, SZU — and Shenzhen — is a fresh start for me. How to find my own heading while, on one side, being handed a collective identity and, on the other, being swept up in the tide of individual fortunes rising and sinking: that will be a long exploration. And at this moment, I stand at the threshold of the torrent.
