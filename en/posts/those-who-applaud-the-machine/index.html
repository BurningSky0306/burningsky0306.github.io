Those Who Applaud the Machine

Those being ground down by the machine often offer its operation the most fervent applause. I try to understand where that applause comes from and explain why I refuse to join in.

In everyday conversation and the clamor of public opinion, I often encounter a dislocation that leaves me stunned. Ordinary people who bear the heavy weight of pressure and discipline in their daily lives light up with genuine excitement and admiration whenever the talk turns to iron-fisted power, grand conquests or rule that cannot be defied. They themselves sit on the conveyor belt of a vast machine, ground down day after day by its gears, yet when they look up at its ruthless operation, they offer their most fervent applause.

This tableau imbued with Cthulhu’s magic — cheering for the very thing that traps them — always sends a deep chill through me. Common sense suggests that those first to feel the ruthlessness of the gears should be the most wary of the machine’s roar. Yet in reality, those who have never held a shred of power become the most devout apologists for coercive power.

I try to hold back easy judgment and understand the psychological mechanism behind this applause. I have to admit that when coercive power manifests as uniform order, swift, decisive efficiency and overwhelming victory, the grand spectacle exerts a dizzying, hypnotic pull. Faced with an unshakable colossus, the small individual easily feels an instinctive awe, unconsciously equating sheer scale with historical inevitability and victorious outcomes with moral legitimacy. Enveloped in a Grand Narrative, people briefly forget the cramped reality of their own lives, mistaking the act of looking up from within the victor’s logic for sharing in a sliver of the ruler’s glory.

Yet the moment I pull my gaze back from that grand vertigo and look again at the specific, exhausted faces around me, the illusion woven from scale and efficiency shatters. On the reverse of every towering monument is carved a brutal record. The ledger of the Grand Narrative counts only the pinnacle that stands at the end, treating the attrition of countless concrete lives beneath the foundation as a necessary cost. Human beings are the highest end in themselves and should never be reduced to mere means. Once we enshrine the triumph of coercive power as our sole measure of value, the dignity crushed under an iron fist, the silenced suffering and the ordinary people burned up as fuel all lose their right to be seen or asked about in the glare of achievement.

I understand how small and powerless an individual is before a vast machine, but I cannot accept painting over scars with praise. Acknowledging that a machine possesses devastating force never means cheering for its act of crushing. The values truly worth defending rest on whether each concrete person is spared the fate of becoming expendable material. Turning my eyes from the dazzling throne to the silent foundation, and holding fast amid the deafening roar to the question of what price individuals pay — this is the only lucidity I can keep in the face of coercive power.
